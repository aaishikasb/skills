# Content angle

Find the article you can substantiate that helps a specific developer make progress.

## Start with the reader and evidence

Read the project, release notes, developer questions, and existing content supplied. Identify the reader's task and starting knowledge, the author's direct experience, the available evidence, and the production constraints. Honor the requested channel and objective; a support-reduction article does not need an invented lead-generation target.

Distinguish a product announcement from a lesson a developer can use. Extract a decision, failure, tradeoff, or working technique supported by the material. Separate what the author observed from a hypothesis, a vendor claim, or an untested extension.

## Choose the premise

Propose only meaningfully different angles. For each, state the reader problem, one-sentence promise, supporting artifact, and what must still be verified. Prefer the angle with useful evidence and a reachable reader outcome over the broadest headline. Recommend one and explain the tradeoff; keep alternatives only if they help the choice.

If differentiation against existing articles or search demand matters, inspect the supplied comparison set or current sources. State exactly what the proposed piece adds: a reproducible example, measured limitation, diagnostic method, or explained decision. Bound novelty claims to what was reviewed. Without research, label the distinction as a hypothesis; do not invent search volume, rankings, competitor coverage, or an empty market.

Keep the headline no broader than its proof. One board, input, and runtime configuration supports a bounded case study, not a universal claim about edge AI. For a failed experiment, the diagnostic method and remaining uncertainty can be the useful contribution.

## Deliver

Produce a brief containing the intended reader, recommended premise and title, evidence-backed takeaway, outline, proof needed for each consequential claim, and exclusions. Order the outline around the reader's problem and the reasoning needed to solve it; use code or diagrams only where they teach something specific.

Choose a relevant success signal, such as completion of a setup task or fewer repeated questions, if measurement is in scope. Treat outcome predictions as hypotheses. The brief is ready for drafting when its central promise has evidence or an explicit research gate. Continue into writing or distribution only if requested.
