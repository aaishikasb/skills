# Talk builder

Build a talk around one idea the audience can use, with a demonstration that advances that idea.

## Establish the format

Read the project, audience, event requirements, slot length, submission constraints, and existing evidence. Distinguish a proposal, outline, script, and slide deck; produce the artifact requested. Ask only about missing constraints that materially affect it. Preserve supplied biography and experience without inventing credentials or results.

Choose one audience takeaway and a concrete opening problem. Build the narrative from a decision or failure, through the mechanism and evidence, to what the audience can apply. Explain prerequisites before the demo depends on them. Cut technical details that do not advance the takeaway.

## Plan timing and proof

Allocate the full slot explicitly, including introduction, transitions, demo setup, audience interaction, recovery buffer, and Q&A if required. Check the total. Keep a planned cut that preserves the main argument when time is lost. Treat timing as estimated until rehearsed aloud.

For each major claim, identify the supporting result or artifact and its limits. Keep simulation, recorded runs, and live target behavior distinct. Showing a successful run demonstrates that instance; it does not establish reliability or production readiness.

For a live demo, specify entry state, visible checkpoint, maximum time spent recovering, a clear trigger for switching, and a labeled fallback. Explain how the story continues after the switch. Rehearsal should exercise both the successful and failed paths; plan required adapters, display legibility, asset availability, and reset steps for the actual venue. Physical demos also need verified operating boundaries and a stopping procedure.

## Deliver

For a proposal, provide the title, abstract within the stated limit, audience/prerequisites, and takeaways. For a talk plan, provide a timed narrative and concise speaker beats, with demo cues and recovery branch. For a script, preserve the speaker's voice and mark stage actions separately. Do not create slides, submit a proposal, or claim a rehearsal happened unless requested and performed.

The artifact is ready for rehearsal when the story teaches the intended takeaway, timing fits, claims have provenance, and losing the demo does not destroy the explanation. Finish with the specific rehearsal checks still needed rather than a claim that the talk is stage-ready.
